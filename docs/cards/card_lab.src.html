Drop2Top card lab · Premier League 2007/08

What is a 2007/08 player worth?

Every number on these cards comes from three verified facts in the Tenaball data: appearances, goals and match results. Nothing is guessed. Pick a club, open a card to see how it was made, and drag the sliders to change what the game values.

Why 2007/08: it is the season D2T's first prototype used, Derby went down with 11 points (the worst squad the game could hand you), and Ronaldo scored 31.

Card anatomy

Six numbers, all from real data

FIFA cards show pace, shooting and dribbling. We have none of that, and inventing it would break the verified-data rule. So the face stats describe what the data does know: did he score, did he matter, did he play, and how good was the team around him.

What matters most to the player

Five things, in order

D2T is a rebuild: you inherit a relegated squad and climb with signings from the same season. Every screen is a comparison, so the card should answer the comparison questions in the order a player asks them.

  1. Overall and position, readable in half a second

    "Is he better than who I have there?" is the question behind every tap. One big number and a position, top left, exactly where FIFA trained everyone to look. If a player has to read six stats to answer it, the card has failed.

  2. Will he actually play?

    Appearances are our most reliable number and the one fans undervalue. A 90-rated player with 9 games is a gamble; FIT makes that visible. In a simulation it can double as injury and availability risk, which gives the stat a real job.

  3. One real headline number

    Derived stats are a game. "31 goals in 34" is a memory. Showing the true season figure on the card (goals for attackers, team goals conceded for defenders and keepers) is what makes a 2007/08 card feel like 2007/08, and it lets fans check us. Tenaball showed that verified numbers are the hook.

  4. A story badge

    Golden Boot, Ever-present, Champion, Relegated, Debut season, Moved mid-season. All computed from the data, all instantly understood. They do the job FIFA's special cards do and give the collection something to chase.

  5. The stats underneath

    GOL, IMP, DEF, WIN and EXP matter for tie-breaks and for the sim, but most players will only read them when two cards are close. Keep them on the card, keep them honest, and let a tap explain each one.

Problems this draft exposes

What the data cannot do yet

Defenders are team ratings

We have no tackles, saves or clean sheets per player, so a defender's DEF is his club's goals conceded, scaled by his games. Wes Brown rates the same as Rio Ferdinand; a good keeper at Derby looks terrible. Per-player clean sheets would fix most of it.

Creators are invisible

No per-season assists, so Fabregas (a famous 2007/08) sits below Gerrard and level with Lampard. Tenaball has Opta assist boards but not every player-season. Gathering assists is the single best data job for the cards.

Experience starts at the Premier League

Torres spent his whole career before this at Atletico Madrid but shows EXP 66 because 2007/08 was his first PL season. Either rename it "PL experience" or add earlier careers.

No ages, no minutes

Apps count a 2-minute sub the same as 90. With no date of birth there is no potential, decline or youth-prospect story, which a multi-season climb will want.

Positions are listings

Positions come from squad lists, so Gareth Barry is a defender and Ronaldo a forward. Good enough for GK/DEF/MID/FWD, not for left back versus centre back.

Data: Tenaball pl_players.csv (two sources per row, goals reconciled for every club-season) and pl_results.csv (the 2007/08 table reproduces Man Utd 87 down to Derby 11). Ratings, fees and badges are this draft's formula and can change. Each player who moved mid-season has one card per club.
