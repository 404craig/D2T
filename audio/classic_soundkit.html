Classic sound kit

Every action and animation in a Classic run, in the order you meet them, with five options each: A, B and C are made in code in three styles (A Soft, B Glass, C Punch; keep to one style if you want the game to sound of a piece), and D and E are recorded with ElevenLabs. Small actions are marked Muted: their sounds stay soft at any volume, and each has a Silent choice. Press ▶ to listen, then Choose, or mark None of these work. Each section ends with a feedback box. The demo strips play whole moments at the game's real timings using your choices.

How these were made: research and approach

What makes game sounds feel good

  • Instant and in time. The sound starts on the frame the thing moves, with a tight front edge, so a tap feels like it caused it.
  • Layers with jobs. A click for the instant, a body for the material (glass, wood, metal), a short tail for space. A single bare tone is what sounds cheap.
  • A hierarchy. The more rewarding the moment, the bigger the sound. Taps stay quiet and short; signings, goals and season ends get the weight.
  • Vary the frequent, fix the signature. Taps and ticks shift pitch a touch each time so repeats don't nag; signature cues stay identical so they're recognised.
  • One key. Every tonal sound here sits in D major (D for D2T), so cues that overlap still sound like one game.

What EA FC and card games do

  • Rarity is felt before it's read. FC signals a card's tier through the reveal itself, and walkouts build in stages (nation, position, club) before the player appears.
  • Tension then release. A rising sound as the reels slow, a beat of quiet, then a distinct hit when the last one lands. Used lightly here: D2T is not a casino.
  • Real stadium under the graphics. Crowd, whistles and nets carry the football; the slick swishes and hits belong to the menus.
  • Match the animation length. A whoosh as long as the swipe, a counter sound as long as the count.

Decision: both, each where it is strongest

  • ElevenLabs REC for real-world textures: crowd, whistles, impacts, fireworks, the tunnel, paper and stamps. It's good at these and poor at crisp clicks.
  • Own synthesis SYN for taps, ticks, notes and anything that must follow an animation frame by frame (reels, table scan, counters) or change by card level. These are generated live, cost nothing to load and can be tuned in code.
  • Mixed MIX for the big moments: a recorded hit under synth notes.
  • 67 ElevenLabs clips made for this kit plus the game's 20, no voices. Each was trimmed so it starts on the first sample.
  • Muted actions (back, tabs, lifting a card) play through their own channel, low-passed and capped, so they never get loud even at full volume.
