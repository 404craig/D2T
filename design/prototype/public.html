DROP2TOP without licences

How a public App Store and Play version could look with no club crests and no real kits, like Golazo: real player and club names, club colours, and all our own art kept. Every screen is the real game (build 225) with the crests swapped out by the game's own data, so each mark is drawn in that club's colours. Switch between the family version and the public one, and between the three replacements.

Version
In place of crests

What changes in the public version

  • Gone: every club crest (cards, pitch cards, shirts, sheets, share images, the faded crest behind screens, home, tables) and the crest plate on the Goal Rush and Unbeaten shirts.
  • Kept: real player names and stats, club names as plain text, club colours, the card levels, the layout, and all our own art: the D2T logo and emblem, the Club X badge, the Goal Rush ball and the Unbeaten mark.
  • The shirts' patterns are already our own generic set (stripes, halves, hoops), not real kits. On the public version they lose their crest plate and stay plain.

The three options

  • A · Strips is the closest to Golazo and the quietest, but clubs with the same colours look identical: Man Utd, Bournemouth and Swindon are all red and white; Derby, Fulham and Newcastle all white and black; Barnsley and Southampton too.
  • B · Roundel is the clearest: every club's 3-letter code is different (MUN, ARS, WHU), so no two clubs ever look the same, even at the tiny size in tables. It reads more as a sticker than a badge.
  • C · Badge looks most like a football badge without copying any real one: the same drawn shield for every club, in its colours, with its 3-letter code. (With two-letter initials six pairs clashed, like Blackburn, Brentford and Brighton, so it uses the codes.)
  • My pick is C for the look or B for clarity: both use the codes, so neither confuses two clubs. A is only safe with something else beside it, like the club's name.

Worth knowing

  • This is a look only. Whether real player names are allowed is the question you're asking FIFPRO.
  • Small text that says "Premier League" as a fact (career goals, top scorers) stays; nothing uses the league's name or lion as branding.
